Predicting Income
From Census Data

A logistic regression on 32,560 people, and a lesson about accuracy.
Tools: Python, pandas, scikit-learn, statsmodels, seaborn.

The question

Can age, hours worked per week and years of education predict whether someone earns more than $50K? The data is the UCI Adult census dataset: 24,719 people at or below $50K and 7,841 above.

What I did

  1. Cleaned text columns (trimmed whitespace, standardized case) and encoded the income label as 0/1.
  2. Explored correlations and plotted the features against income class.
  3. Fit a logistic regression in statsmodels for interpretation (odds ratios) and in scikit-learn with a 70/30 train/test split for prediction.
  4. Evaluated with a confusion matrix, precision, recall and F1.

Results

MeasureIncome at or below 50KIncome above 50K
Precision0.770.44
Recall0.960.09
F10.850.15

Overall accuracy was 75.1% on 9,768 test rows. That sounds decent, but about 76% of people earn $50K or less, so a model that always guessed "at or below 50K" scores about the same. The model finds only 221 of the 2,373 high earners in the test set.

What I learned and fixed

  • Accuracy can mislead on imbalanced data. I now compare against a majority-class baseline and report recall for the minority class.
  • A coding mistake changed a feature. My first version overwrote the original years-of-education column with arbitrary category codes, which made the education result meaningless. I caught it when the odds ratio contradicted my earlier run, and the notebook now keeps the original column.
  • Next steps: class weighting (added to the notebook), more features such as occupation and capital gains, and a tree-based comparison model.

Numbers above come from the original run. After the fix the notebook needs to be re-run, and these figures may change.
