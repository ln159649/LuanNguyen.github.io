Who Buys Bikes?

Cleaning 1,000 customer records and finding which groups are most likely to buy.
Tools: Excel, pivot tables, slicers, dashboard.

The question

A bike shop has data on 1,000 customers. Which customer characteristics go with buying a bike, so marketing can focus on the right groups?

What I did

  1. Cleaned the raw sheet: removed duplicate rows (26 duplicates removed, 1,026 raw rows became 1,000), standardized labels (for example M/S to Married/Single, M/F to Male/Female).
  2. Added an age bracket column (0-30, 31-54, 55+).
  3. Built three pivot tables with slicers and a final one-page dashboard.

Findings

  • 48.1% of customers bought a bike (481 of 1,000).
  • Cars owned matters. Customers with 0 or 1 car bought at 61% and 57%. With 2 or more cars the rate drops to 36-39%.
  • Middle-aged customers buy most. 54% of ages 31-54 bought, compared with 36% of those 30 and under and 32% of those 55 and over.
  • Short and mid commutes buy more. Customers commuting under a mile (55%) or 2-5 miles (59%) bought more than those commuting 10+ miles (30%).
  • Income barely matters. Purchase rates were 47%, 51% and 47% across low, middle and high income groups, so targeting by income alone would not help.
  • By region, the Pacific region led (59%) versus North America (43%).

Caveats

These are rates from a sample of 1,000. Some groups are small (for example, 59 customers with 4 cars), and the data shows association only. The dashboard in the workbook shows the same pivots as interactive charts.
